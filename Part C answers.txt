Why does replication need total ordering for conflicting operations? Use a concrete example
Basically if we don't have total ordering, the replicas might apply updates in different orders and end up
with a completely different data. An example of this is if a bank account has $100 and if there are 2 seperate
updates that happen at the same time. if replica 1 adds $10 and replica 2 adds 10%, replica 1 makes the balance
become $121 but if we switch the order of replica 1 and replica 2 the balance becomes $120. the importance of total
ordering is to make sure they both apply at the same exact time in the exact same sequence so the data matches.

What do Lamport clocks guarantee and what do they not guarantee?
If event A causes event B, A will get a smaller timestamp, when you add a tie-breaker they basically make the whole
system agree on one strict timeline. They don't guarantee any tracking of real-world time.

Your algorithm assumes reliable FIFO communication. What breaks if messages can be lost or delivered out of FIFO order?
if the messages are lost then the system goes into deadlock, so the replicas will be sitting there forever. If the
delivery is out of order then the system ruins the data. In this case a replica make get a newer message first and assume
it's safe to process the queue but accidentally apply an old message that finally arives to the node.

Where is the "Coordination" happening in your implementation (middleware vs application logic)
the coordination happens in the middleware as that does all the heavy lifting. the application logic is just listening
to the middleware and updates whenever the middleware says to update.
